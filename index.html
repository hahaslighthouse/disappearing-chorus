Hi! This is Haha

Quantum Chess got me wondering what else we could play with to understand superposition and measurement. I landed on something you can hear: your own voice.

One voice, many possibilities

In quantum physics, a state can be a combination of several possible outcomes. That’s a superposition. Try splitting your melody here. The two new voices each get half their parent’s weight, and you can hear them together. The percentages set their volumes in this demo.

What happens when you measure?

A quantum measurement gives one outcome. The state determines how likely each outcome is. Here, Measure picks one voice using the same percentages and brings it back to full volume. A voice marked 25% has a one-in-four chance of being the one you hear afterward.

Sound is our analogy. Real quantum states also carry phase, which lets possibilities interfere and change the odds.

Why is Einstein following your mouse?

I drew him by hand just for this site. Einstein questioned whether quantum mechanics gave a complete picture of reality, and I like having that curiosity follow you around as you play. Read the 1935 paper.
